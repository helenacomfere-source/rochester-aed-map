About this map

An independent community-verified map of AEDs in Rochester, built in partnership with AEDs for Athletes.

The problem

Cardiac events happen without warning, in gyms, on fields, in school hallways and in ordinary public buildings. Survival depends on how fast someone finds a defibrillator. But in most communities, nobody knows where the AEDs are until they need one — and by then there is no time to look.

Rochester is a clear example. When we checked OpenStreetMap, the largest open mapping database in the world, it listed one AED in the entire city, and 35 across all of Minnesota. Those AEDs exist. They are simply not written down anywhere the public can reach.

What we do about it

This map collects AED locations from the community and confirms them in person. Every record shows how it was sourced and when it was last checked, so you can tell the difference between an AED we stood in front of last month and one somebody mentioned two years ago.

Verified means verified

A pin on a map is easy. A pin you can trust in an emergency is not. We would rather show fewer AEDs and be honest about what we know than show a large map that sends someone running toward a defibrillator that is not there.

How this connects to AEDs for Athletes

AEDs for Athletes exists to educate communities about sudden cardiac arrest and to place AEDs where they are missing. This map extends that mission in both directions: it helps people find the devices that already exist, and the gaps it reveals show us where the next donated AED should go.

It also widens the audience. The organization's work centers on athletes and young people, but a map serves everyone — coaches, parents, teachers, and older adults across the whole Rochester community.

Help us fill in the map

If you know where an AED is, add it. Schools, sports complexes, community centers and places of worship are the highest priority. Every submission is reviewed before it appears as verified.

Who built this

This map was built by Helena Comfere, a student and a member of AEDs for Athletes Rochester. It is an independent project made to support the organization's work, not an official product of it — the AEDs, the verification visits, and the data behind it are the part that matters, and those are done on the ground here in Rochester.

Questions, corrections, or an AED to add? Get in touch.

About AEDs for Athletes

AEDs for Athletes is a youth-led 501(c)(3) fostering a culture of preparedness by bringing CPR and AED readiness into every community. The organization is dedicated to building that culture through CPR, AED, and emergency response education in youth sports — educating students, coaches, and community members, and helping place AEDs where they are missing.

"To make sure the next time, we're ready — not lucky."

The organization was founded in Rochester, Minnesota and has partnered with the National Federation of State High School Associations on educational materials. Its approach centers on empowering young people to learn and lead in emergency preparedness rather than waiting for adults to provide all the answers.

As a registered 501(c)(3) charitable organization, donations to AEDs for Athletes are tax-deductible to the extent allowed by law.

This map is an independent, community-verified project built in partnership with AEDs for Athletes. "Community-verified" means exactly what it says: locations are submitted by members of the public and confirmed in person by volunteers. It does not mean the information has been certified by a government agency, an emergency services provider, or a medical authority.

What this site is — and what it is not

Always call 911 first

This website is not a substitute for calling 911. In a cardiac emergency, call 911 immediately and follow the dispatcher's instructions. Do not delay calling in order to look up an AED on this site. Emergency dispatchers can often direct you to the nearest defibrillator themselves.

We cannot guarantee accuracy

AED locations on this map are provided for general awareness and community education. They are gathered from public sources and community submissions. AEDs can be moved, removed, locked away, run out of battery, or be otherwise unavailable without notice. We cannot guarantee that any AED shown here is present, accessible, or in working order at any given moment.

This is why every record shows how it was sourced and when it was last checked. Treat the map as a planning tool to learn your surroundings in advance — not as a live inventory to rely on during an emergency.

Not medical advice or training

Information on this site, including the guidance on what to do in a cardiac emergency, is general educational information only. It is not medical advice, and reading it is not equivalent to certified CPR or AED training. It does not create any clinician–patient relationship.

We do not write our own medical guidance. All health and safety information on this site is drawn from national health authorities and emergency-care organizations — primarily the American Heart Association, the American Red Cross, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, and the American Academy of Pediatrics — and each claim links to its source. See where this information comes from. Where our wording and theirs differ, theirs is correct.

AEDs for Athletes provides CPR and AED education, but is not a certifying body and does not issue CPR certification. For certification, contact an accredited provider such as the American Red Cross, the American Heart Association, or a local hospital.

Not an official AED registry

Minnesota law requires owners of public-access AEDs to register them with an AED registry within 30 days of acquisition. This site is a community awareness tool and is not a legal AED registry. Adding an AED here does not satisfy that requirement. AED owners should register with the Minnesota AED Registry or another recognized registry.

Privacy

If you submit an AED, you may optionally provide an email address. It is used only to follow up with questions about that specific AED, and is never published on the map, sold, or shared. Do not submit personal information about anyone other than yourself.

Corrections and removals

If a location is wrong, out of date, or should not be listed — including if you own or manage a property and want its AED removed from the map — get in touch and we will update or remove it promptly.
